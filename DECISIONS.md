# Prototype decisions

The prototype must work in 20–30 days, so it departs from the long-term spec in the places below. Each row says what we did, why, and when to revisit it. "Spec" means the documents in the "Proyct IA" project.

| # | Decision | Spec said | Why | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One Python service (Starlette) plus a static React app | TypeScript monorepo, tRPC, several services | One process to build, run and deploy in 30 days; the vision and render code is Python anyway | After the prototype, if it grows |
| 2 | SQLite and local disk | Postgres + pgvector, S3 | No infrastructure to stand up; ~90 finishes need no vector search | First real deployment with several servers |
| 3 | In-process jobs with numbered progress events over SSE | Redis + BullMQ queue | Same customer experience, no queue to operate | When jobs must survive restarts |
| 4 | Kitchens and Kober countertops first | Bathrooms first | The catalogue is Kober (countertops, Spläsh, sinks) | — |
| 5 | Countertops quoted in standard pieces (2.4 / 3.0 / 3.6 m, 1.2 / 1.8 m for Estilo) | Area × price | Kober sells whole pieces; area pricing would misquote | — |
| 6 | Areas stored as integer mm² (`area_mm2`) | `area_m2` in the data model | Resolves the conflict with CLAUDE.md's integer rule | — |
| 7 | Suggestion schema: title, countertop finish, profile, backsplash finish, reason. No coverage, waste or price | `SurfaceSelection` carried coverage and waste | The model must not influence quantities | — |
| 8 | Scale always comes from typed run lengths; photo-only scale is never quoted | No rule for photo-only scale | Monocular scale is not accurate enough to price | After the geometry eval |
| 9 | Deterministic render only; no generative pass yet | Low-strength depth-conditioned pass | The composite already looks photographic; hosted edit models offer no strength or depth control | Week 3: optional Qwen-Image-Edit polish, product pixels pasted back |
| 10 | Models: SAM 3 on fal.ai for surfaces, Claude Sonnet 5.5 for description and suggestions, offline mocks for tests | SAM 2 + labelling head, Depth Anything V2, Claude Opus 5 | Current, commercially licensed, hosted (no GPUs to run) | Self-host SAM 3 / Qwen when volume justifies |
| 11 | Anonymous owner cookie instead of accounts | Auth provider | No sign-up friction for a demo; still scopes every query | Before real customers pay online |
| 12 | 10% allowance on every run | Waste factor per surface class, a business decision | As instructed on 28 Sep; now a cell in the team's sheet | Confirm the countertop allowance with the field team |
| 13 | Spanish UI first, English toggle | English copy | Mexican market (Kober, MXN) | Confirm market |
| 14 | Visit request without online payment | Stripe booking fee | Payments are Phase 5 of the long plan; the fee rule is in row 35 | After the pilot |
| 15 | One Claude call per step, with the whole Kober list in the prompt, answering through structured outputs (JSON schema) | Tool-using design agent (catalogue search, SKU fetch, surface list) | 78 finishes fit in one prompt, so no search tools are needed; Claude Sonnet 5.5 rejects forced tool use and a non-default temperature | Bring the tools back if the catalogue grows past a few hundred items |
| 16 | Four fixed style presets (Minimalista, Cálido natural, Contraste, Creativo) and a one-click board of four finished designs | Six curated themes, one design per request | Compare design ideas on the customer's own kitchen in one step; each board design is an ordinary design, so pricing and invariants are unchanged | After pilot feedback on which styles people pick |
| 17 | Lovable look: cream canvas, ink pill buttons, Poppins; the clay accent marks selection, hotspots and the visit button | Clay fills every primary button; Inter | Matches the reference the team chose; Poppins ships in the app (OFL, Latin subset, about 12 KB per weight) | Confirm type and palette against the reference |
| 18 | Light theme on every screen except the design review and the shared view, which are always dark | Other screens follow the system setting | One theme to polish in 30 days | After the pilot |
| 19 | Hotspot washes of 0.12 on hover and 0.18 when selected, with a white-over-dark outline | 0.16 and 0.26 | 0.26 turned dark finishes visibly brown; the outline carries the selection | Re-check on 10 real renders |
| 20 | Prices come from one workbook the team fills in (Google Sheet or .xlsx): Kober's cost per piece in Original Q by category x width x length, a factor per profile, Spläsh panel costs, a margin per category, labour, fees, IVA, allowance, finish categories and availability; imported into `prices.json` with status BORRADOR or VIGENTE | Catalogue read model over the company's product services | There is no product service yet, and every number is a business decision; one sheet the team already knows how to edit, validated on import, keeps them in charge and keeps all arithmetic in `quote.py` | When Kober sends its price list format, or a product service exists |
| 21 | The team imports the sheet from the staff page by pasting its Google Sheets link (shared "anyone with the link can view") | — | No deploy or command line needed to change a price; a failed import lists what to fix and keeps the old prices | Use the Sheets API with a service account if the sheet must stay private |
| 22 | Staff view behind one shared team key; staff photos and renders through signed links that expire after 12 h | Ops console with accounts | Enough for one field team in a pilot; no key ever appears in a URL | Before a second team, or before anyone outside the company uses it |
| 23 | The visit record re-prices the design with the team's measurements as a final quote (`final=True`), keeps the signed difference per run and whether the final total fell inside the range the customer saw; the customer's own design keeps its estimate | Phase 5: SiteVisit with VerifiedMeasurement, recompute, show deltas, take the balance | The measurement-accuracy metric starts with the first visit, as MEASUREMENT.md asks, before payments exist | Phase 5: show the change table to the customer and take the balance |
| 24 | Share links: one read-only link per design, showing the render, finishes and quote, never the customer's name, phone or project | — | Customers send designs to family on WhatsApp; the link can't be used to reach anything else | Add expiry or revoke if customers ask |
| 25 | Printable quote is a page laid out for A4 that the browser prints or saves as PDF | — | No PDF library on the server; the same page serves the customer and the team's final quote | If the team needs PDFs attached to emails automatically |
| 26 | Repo rebuilt on 2 Oct from the project's text copy: finish images are drawn stand-ins (`make_placeholder_swatches.py`, flagged in the app), the sample photo is rebuilt from the saved comparison grid, the golden render and demo images were regenerated from them | Real swatches from the Kober PDF | The PDF and the earlier zip weren't available to the rebuild | Run `make catalogue PDF=...`, then regenerate the golden image and the demo |
| 27 | Settings read without pydantic-settings; HTTP through httpx2 or httpx | pydantic-settings, httpx | The rebuild environment could not install them; both paths are tested | — |
| 28 | Countertop depth is a product size, not a measurement: a run on standard base cabinets is a 645 mm Kober piece, and the customer is never asked for the depth. Narrow runs (450 / 500 / 550 mm) and islands or bars (700–1000 mm) are chosen as a type, not measured; the visit confirms. Only run lengths are measured | Typed length, width and height for every surface | Base cabinets in Mexico are about 600 mm deep and the counter overhangs them, so 645 mm fits almost every kitchen; Kober prices a piece by width × length, so a fixed width leaves length as the only number that changes the price, and the price only steps at whole piece lengths | If visits show many kitchens off the standard width |
| 29 | Lengths are never estimated from a photo, including from objects of known size in it (counter depth, stove width) | Reference-object scale held as research, `research_only` | A length inferred from "typical sizes" is a guess; it can't carry a price, even as a range | — |
| 30 | Renderer `composite-v3`: the countertop is drawn as a slab. Its front edge and visible ends are their own faces at the chosen profile's thickness, the pattern wrapping over the edge, shaded as vertical faces with the profile's shape (rounded Original and Basi-K, square otherwise). Faces are found inside the detected mask; for a hand-drawn outline the edge band is predicted below the front line and snapped to the old edge's bottom line in the photo. Light far above the surface's median (a soft knee at 1.3x) is treated as the old surface's shine and added back as light scaled by the new finish's gloss | Edge profile changed the price only | v2 renders looked paper-thin and copied a dark glossy counter's glare onto light finishes as haze | When the model test run shows Marigold-IID's lighting or MoGe-2's geometry beat these |
| 31 | Objects on and in front of the surfaces: Claude lists everything it sees (open list, in the same call as the kitchen description), SAM 3 segments each one plus the sink, cooktop and faucet, and the product masks are the surfaces minus the objects. The object list is kept with the photo for pointers | Two surface prompts only | SAM 3 finds only what it is named; a fixed list would miss real objects and paint over them (quality first, CLAUDE.md) | Self-hosting SAM 3 makes the per-object prompts free |
| 32 | The camera's focal length comes from the photo's EXIF when it passes the checks (Apple zoom already included, other makers' digital zoom applied, 12-120 mm, not cropped), else a 26 mm-equivalent default; it shapes the render only, never a length | Fixed 26 mm guess | Ultra-wide 0.5x photos are common in small kitchens and were mis-modelled | Add GeoCalib as the fallback if the model test run supports it |
| 33 | Candidate models (MoGe-2, GeoCalib, Marigold-IID-Lighting) are tried in a GitHub Actions test run on CPU with free-licence kitchen photos before any is adopted; results go to the `model-eval-results` branch | — | No GPU here and no published benchmark on kitchen countertops; adopt on evidence | — |
| 34 | The room's light comes from Marigold-IID-Lighting v1-1 (diffuse shading without the surfaces' colour or shine), run on our own model worker (`workers/gpu`: a cloud GPU billed per second that stops when idle, or a Mac); the renderer's own estimate stays as the fallback when the worker is unreachable. Offline, the sample photo uses Marigold's stored output, like its stored masks | Light estimated from the photo's brightness | The model test run showed Marigold separates light from the old counter's colour and glare, and renders with it look more natural; its licence (RAIL++-M) allows commercial use; no hosted API exists, so we host it | If a better commercially licensed lighting model appears |
| 35 | The measuring visit is free when the customer hires us for the installation; otherwise it costs one flat fee from the price sheet (`cuota_visita`, placeholder $500 + IVA = $580). Quotes carry it as `visit_fee` with the rule `free_if_hired`; there is no deposit and no balance | Stripe booking fee, then the balance after the visit | The team owns the installation business (team decision, 6 Oct); the visit is part of selling the job | When the team sets the real fee in the sheet |
| 36 | Price tags on the render: one labelled pointer per priced thing (each countertop run, its edge, each backsplash, the sink fitting), placed at the surface by the renderer (`manifest.pointers`). A tag shows the item's share of the quote with IVA, taken from `quote.items`, which add up to the total exactly; the edge opens the other profiles this finish is made in, with the difference, and renders the chosen one | Numbered pins showing the finish name only | Customers asked "what is this and what does it cost" of each thing they see; the pointer carries no price, so the quote stays the only source | When the builder lets customers change items in place |
| 37 | Price comparison with Home Depot dropped | Compare against retail prices | Home Depot sells Kober pieces but doesn't measure or install; it isn't the competition (team decision, 6 Oct) | — |
| 38 | The model worker runs on Google Cloud Run: one NVIDIA L4 without zonal redundancy, the minimum 4 vCPU / 16 GiB, none when idle, at most one instance; weights baked into the image; deployed from GitHub with Workload Identity Federation (no key files), the token in Secret Manager | Modal | The team already has a Google Cloud account; Cloud Run bills per second and scales to zero; AWS's serverless inference has no GPUs. About US$1.05 an hour while running (us-central1, Oct 2026) | Raise max instances with traffic; compare with a Mac mini once volume is steady |
